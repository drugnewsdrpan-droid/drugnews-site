# GLP-1 and Luxury Hospitality | Primary Sources and Boundaries

1. [Journal of Marketing Research | The No-Hunger Games](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222437251412834): supports the approximately 150,000-household panel, the matched analysis of 2,602 GLP-1-adopting households, and the six-month changes in grocery, higher-income household and limited-service dining spending. This is a U.S. observational transaction study, not a randomized trial or a forecast of global hotel revenue.
2. [Forbes Travel Guide | How GLP-1s Are Reshaping Luxury Hospitality](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbestravelguide/2026/07/15/how-glp-1s-are-reshaping-luxury-hospitality/): supports the first-wave examples involving St. Regis Washington, D.C., The Ned Doha, The Broadmoor, Miami Beach EDITION, Four Seasons Fort Lauderdale, Omni La Costa and Hilton Head Health. It is industry reporting and operator commentary, not a hotel-industry census.
3. [U.S. FDA | Wegovy 2025 Prescribing Information](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/215256s023lbl.pdf): supports delayed gastric emptying and common gastrointestinal adverse reactions observed in adult weight-management trials. One product label does not establish identical effects for every GLP-1 medicine or user.
4. [PwC | 2026 GLP-1 Consumer Trends](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/consumer-markets/library/glp-1-consumer-trends.html): supports the estimate that about 21% of U.S. households had a current user as of May 2026 and the survey-reported shifts in sweets, salty snacks, produce and protein foods. These are U.S. data and do not establish global causality.
5. [JAMA Psychiatry | Once-Weekly Semaglutide in Adults With Alcohol Use Disorder](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2829811): supports early signals from a 48-person, nine-week Phase 2 randomized trial. Not every alcohol endpoint was significant, and the study does not establish semaglutide as a proven treatment for alcohol use disorder.
6. [The Obesity Society and partner organizations | Nutritional Priorities to Support GLP-1 Therapy](https://www.obesity.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Joint_Nutritional-Priorities-to-Support-GLP-1-Therapy-for-Obesity.pdf): supports attention to nutrient density, adequate protein and resistance training. It does not validate hotel IV drips or beauty treatments.
7. [Hilton Head Health | GLP-1 Support Experience](https://www.hhhealth.com/glp-1-support-experience/): documents a commercial stay combining chef-prepared meals, protein guidance, strength and recovery classes, dietitian consultation and cooking workshops; it does not establish clinical outcomes.
8. [Four Seasons Los Angeles | Immortelle](https://www.fourseasons.com/losangeles/landing-pages/property/immortelle/) and [Seasonal Treatments](https://www.fourseasons.com/losangeles/spa/seasonal-treatments/): document third-party IV and wellness services, including $750 and $1,000 packages. The hotel states that the provider is a third party; commercial descriptions are not clinical efficacy evidence.
9. [Nestlé USA | Vital Pursuit](https://www.nestleusa.com/media/pressreleases/vital-pursuit-glp-1): documents the positioning of portion-controlled, protein-forward and fiber-containing products for GLP-1 users and weight-management consumers; it does not prove market size or commercial success.

## Fixed Boundaries

- A first wave of hotel experiments is not an industry-wide transformation.
- Larger spending declines among higher-income households do not mean every luxury traveler will spend less.
- Growth in zero-proof menus is a demand test, not evidence that GLP-1 medicines are established alcohol-use treatments.
- Hotels may integrate nutrition, exercise and accommodation, but commercial wellness claims cannot substitute for clinical evidence or blur medical accountability.
